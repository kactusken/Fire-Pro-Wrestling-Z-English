scene scripts (`tools/storyflow.py` decodes them; the format is in docs/TECHNICAL.md "Story

been confirmed in play yet. Names are the real-name build's (factions and titles too: Team 2000, No Fear, BATT, PRIDE, WAR,

## How story mode works

Each scenario follows one wrestler through a career told in scenes: dialogue, matches you play,
and now and then a question with two or three answers. A hidden **story score** decides most
routes: some scenes check it ("score > 15") and send you one way or the other. Match results decide
the rest, and in a few places the answer you picked does.

**The score** starts at 0 and never drops below 0. It rises three ways:
1. **Winning a match**: most match scenes add +1 or +2 for a win (the *win points* column in the
   tables). A result counts as a win when the game reports a result under 2.
2. **Some answers**: a few questions give points (listed in each scenario's decisions).
3. **Playing a good match**: after every match you play, win or lose, the game adds up to 3:
   - +1 if the match lasted 8 to 15 minutes (match clock);
   - +1 if you landed 1 to 3 hits of a special kind (hits flagged 0x2000 - probably criticals);
   - +1 if you finished with a little more stamina left than your opponent (ahead by under 8191
     of 65535), or with almost all of it left while the opponent has almost none (ahead by more
     than 61425); nothing if you finished behind.
   *(From the code: the match native, 0x1E9ECC. Which wrestler slot is yours and what the 0x2000
   hits are is not confirmed in play.)*

So the scripted points alone are often far below a threshold: Misawa's NOAH route needs a score
over 30 while the scenes give at most 25. Long, close, well-fought matches are what open the
"high" routes. Scenario 7 plays no matches (you pick Win or Lose), so its score never rises.

Thresholds are strict: "score > 15" means 16 or more. A new scenario starts with the score and
every flag at 0; loading a story save restores them.

Each scenario has its own match numbering (the `game(n)` number in the script). Match results are
kept, but no scene checks an earlier match's result: each result only picks the scene right after
the match.

## Developer chapter menus

Each scenario's script starts with a chapter-select menu (`misawa000`, `oonita000`/`000a`/`000b`,
...: two to six pages) that normal play never shows; its first entry is the scenario's first scene.
Its captions are the developers' own chapter names ("014 The End of Heresy", "113 vs Chono",
"PRIDE Route", "Batroi" for battle royal) and were translated with the rest. Jumping into a chapter
from it starts with the score and flags as they are, which is how otherwise hidden scenes (Liger's
G1 final) can be seen. How to open the menu in game is not known.
